This project performs exploratory data analysis (EDA) on a publicly available Fashion Retail Sales dataset relevant to the apparel and textiles retail sector. The objective is to identify patterns in product purchases, transaction values, monthly sales, customer ratings and payment preferences, and to translate the findings into practical business insights. The analysis is implemented in Python using Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib and Seaborn.

The workflow starts by loading the CSV file and examining its dimensions, columns, data types, missing values and duplicate records. Column names are standardized, and purchase amounts, review ratings and purchase dates are converted to suitable data types. Duplicate transactions are removed. Records without a usable item, purchase date or payment method are excluded because these fields are needed for the planned analysis. Missing purchase amounts are filled using the median, while missing review ratings are filled using the mean when valid ratings are available. Potential purchase-amount outliers are identified with the 1.5-times-interquartile-range rule and flagged for review rather than automatically deleted.

The script produces six visualizations: the ten most frequently purchased items, the ten items with the highest total purchase amount, monthly purchase amount trends, payment-method distribution, review rating versus purchase amount, and a box plot of purchase amounts. It also exports cleaned data and summary tables for item performance, monthly sales and payment methods. These outputs can support merchandising decisions, stock-planning discussions, customer-experience reviews and payment convenience analysis.

Business recommendations are based on the actual output generated after running the script on the downloaded CSV. The dataset does not include inventory, product costs, profit margins, marketing expenditure, store locations or detailed customer demographics. Therefore, purchase amount must not be described as profit, and correlation must not be interpreted as causation. The project is reproducible and includes instructions for running it in Visual Studio Code.
